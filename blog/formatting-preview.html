A note on randomness

How averaging changes uncertainty.

Formatting preview | 2 min read

Take 100 independent draws from a standard normal distribution and average them. Repeat this a thousand times. The averages still vary, but they cluster much closer to zero than the individual draws.

The standard deviation drops by a factor of ten. Write the sample mean as

X¯n=1n∑i=1nXi

When the observations are independent and have a common variance, averaging reduces that variance by a factor of n.1 The simulation below makes that change visible.

Var(X¯n)=σ2n

A small experiment

This Python example takes the average of 100 draws, then repeats the experiment 1,000 times. A fixed seed makes the results reproducible.

from random import Random

rng = Random(7)
means = [
    sum(
        rng.gauss(0, 1)
        for _ in range(100)
    ) / 100
    for _ in range(1_000)
]

The values in means will cluster more tightly around zero than the individual draws. Increasing the number of observations makes the average more stable.
